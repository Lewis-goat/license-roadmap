How to Become a Notary in Texas (2026): Cost, Steps, Timeline

Last updated: September 13, 2026

Texas is one of the easiest and cheapest states in the country to become a notary public: no exam, no mandatory course, a $21 filing fee, and a four-year commission. This guide walks the exact process, the real costs, and the rules that make new Texas notaries different from other states (including the record-book requirement — Texas is stricter than most).

Who qualifies

Per the Texas Secretary of State, you must:

  • Be at least 18 years old
  • Be a Texas resident (or a resident of a bordering state who is regularly employed in Texas, or a Texas-based business entity)
  • Be a US citizen or permanent legal resident (this is a Texas-specific requirement — several states don't have it)
  • Not have been convicted of a felony or a crime involving moral turpitude (final convictions are disqualifying; details in the state's eligibility rules)

The step-by-step

1. Get the $10,000 surety bond. Texas requires a four-year $10,000 surety bond. Bonding agencies (including the state-bar-endorsed ones and national providers) charge roughly $30–$70 for the full term. The bond protects the public, not you — for your own protection, consider errors-and-omissions (E&O) coverage, sold separately.

2. Apply with the $21 filing fee. Two routes: online through the Secretary of State's SOSDirect system (fastest), or by mail with the paper form. The application asks for your bond information and eligibility attestations. Bonding agencies will file on your behalf if you buy the bond through them — the bundle is why most people use an agency.

3. Receive your commission and buy your tools. Processing typically runs about 10 business days for complete online filings. Once your commission certificate arrives you may notarize — after acquiring the two Texas-mandated tools:

  • A notary seal/stamp (must meet the state's size and wording specifications — agencies sell compliant ones)
  • A record book (journal) — Texas requires every notarial act to be entered in a bound record book, a rule many states don't have. This is the #1 compliance point new Texas notaries miss.

4. Know your Texas-specific rules. The ones with teeth: fees are capped ($6 per notarial act standard, $25 for oaths/affidavits-related service — the Secretary of State publishes the current fee schedule), the journal is mandatory, you must notarize only within Texas (with narrow bordering-state exceptions), and identification requirements are specific (current, government-issued, with photo and signature — or personal knowledge).

Realistic total cost

Item Typical cost
$10,000 surety bond (4-year term) $30–$70
State filing fee $21
Seal + record book $20–$60
Optional E&O insurance $15–$100+
Realistic all-in ~$100–$150

Timeline

Day 1: buy bond + submit application → roughly 10 business days to commission → same week: order seal and journal (a few days) → you're notarizing within about three weeks of deciding to start. That makes Texas notary one of the fastest legitimate credentials in the country.

Making money with it

The commission itself is capped-fee ($6/act), but the Texas notary credential is the gateway to loan signing agent work (mortgage document packages, paid per signing, not per act) — a fee-schedule niche where notaries who complete signing-agent training earn substantially more per appointment. That's a training-and-marketing question, not a licensing one: the state credential is the same.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Fees and rules change — verify current requirements at the Texas Secretary of State's notary page before applying.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to become a notary in Texas?

The state filing fee is $21 for a four-year commission. You also need a $10,000 surety bond (typically $30–$70 for the term through a bonding agency), a notary seal/stamp and record book (roughly $20–$60 combined), and optional errors-and-omissions insurance. A realistic all-in total through a bonding agency is about $100–$150.

How long does a Texas notary commission last?

Four years from the effective date, renewable. Renewal follows the same application-and-bond process — Texas does not require an exam or education course for the standard notary commission.

Do I need to take an exam or a course to become a Texas notary?

No exam and no mandatory course for the standard four-year commission. Texas requires eligibility (18+, US citizen or permanent resident, Texas resident or qualifying business presence), a $10,000 surety bond, and the application with the $21 fee. Optional training is available and useful for avoiding certificate mistakes.

How long does approval take?

Applications filed online through the Secretary of State's system or via a bonding agency are typically processed within about 10 business days once everything is on file; paper filings take longer. Your commission certificate and required records arrive by the channel you selected when filing.
