HVAC License Requirements by State (2026 Guide)

HVAC licensing confuses people because it's really three different questions wearing one coat: (1) what does the federal government require? (2) what does my state require of contractors? (3) what does my state require of technicians? Answer them in that order and the map makes sense.

Layer 1 — The federal rule that applies everywhere

Under the Clean Air Act, anyone who maintains, services, repairs, or disposes of equipment containing regulated refrigerants must hold EPA Section 608 certification. This is universal — every state, every employer, no apprenticeship exemption. Four exam sections (Core plus Type I, II, III; pass all and you're Universal), and under federal rules the certification does not expire. Full breakdown: EPA 608 certification explained.

Layer 2 — State contractor licensing (most states)

The business layer: if you sign contracts, pull permits, or operate as an HVAC company, nearly every state requires a mechanical/HVAC contractor license, typically with:

  • Documented experience (commonly 2–5 years, sometimes with a licensed-contractor supervision period)
  • A trade exam + a business-and-law exam (administrators like PSI/Prometric)
  • Insurance, bonding, and registration

Examples of the pattern: Florida licenses Class A (unlimited), B (limited tonnage) and C contractors through DBPR; Texas licenses through TDLR. Verify your state's board — the agency page is always current.

Layer 3 — Technician licensing (state-by-state split)

For working as an employee, states split:

  • No technician license (the majority): you work under a licensed contractor; EPA 608 is your credential.
  • Journeyman/technician licensing: some states license the worker tier too — with hour-plus-exam structures similar to electricians.

If you plan to be an employee, Layer 1 plus a willing employer gets you working in most states; if you plan to run jobs, Layer 2 is your license.

How to verify your state in five minutes

  1. Search your state + HVAC license and click the .gov result (state contractor board or licensing department).
  2. Answer three questions from that page: Does the state license technicians or only contractors? What experience counts? Which exams?
  3. Note the worker-registration rule (some states require apprentices/helpers to register even without a license).

The realistic path most people take

  1. EPA 608 first (weeks, low cost — it's employable on day one).
  2. Trade school certificate or paid helper job (6–12 months of fundamentals).
  3. Work under a licensed contractor, logging hours.
  4. Contractor license when you're ready to run jobs (see how to become an HVAC technician for the early steps).

General information, not career or legal advice. Rules change and vary by state — verify with your state contractor board before working or contracting. Federal refrigerant rules: EPA Section 608.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a license to do HVAC work?

It splits into two layers: handling refrigerants requires federal EPA Section 608 certification everywhere in the US, no exceptions. Beyond that, most states require a license for HVAC contractors (the business that signs contracts and pulls permits), while technician-level employment often needs no state license at all. A handful of states also license journeyman-level HVAC technicians.

Which federal certification does every HVAC tech need?

EPA Section 608 certification, required under the Clean Air Act for anyone who maintains, services, repairs or disposes of equipment containing regulated refrigerants. It has four exam sections (Core, Type I, Type II, Type III; passing all three types earns Universal) and, once earned, does not expire under federal rules.

Do HVAC apprentices need a license?

Usually no state license at the helper/apprentice stage — though some states (e.g., those with registration systems like Texas's for certain trades) require registration, and you still need EPA 608 before touching refrigerants. Check your state's contractor board for the worker-registration rule.

Is HVAC contracting licensed in every state?

Nearly all — states like Florida (Class A/B/C contractors through DBPR) and Texas (TDLR) run full contractor licensing with experience requirements, exams, insurance and bonding. The exceptions and local quirks are exactly why the state-by-state verification step exists.
