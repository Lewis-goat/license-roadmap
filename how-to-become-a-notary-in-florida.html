How to Become a Notary in Florida (2026): $89 Steps, No Exam

Florida sits in the sweet spot for easy state credentials: no exam, a mandatory-but-short education course, and a total cost around $90. The state is also one of the busiest notary markets in America (retirees, real estate, and a large Spanish-speaking population drive enormous signing volume). Here's the exact path for a first-time Florida notary.

Who qualifies

Per Florida law, you must:

  • Be at least 18 years old
  • Be a legal resident of Florida (this is strict — Florida is one of the few states requiring permanent residency, not just a business presence)
  • Be able to read, write, and understand English
  • Not have a felony conviction (with a narrow civil-rights-restoration exception process through the state's clemency system)

The step-by-step

1. Take the mandatory 3-hour education course. First-time applicants must complete the state-approved interactive Notary Education Course — Florida's Department of State offers one online, and private providers sell equivalents (roughly $25, sometimes bundled free with a bond purchase). The certificate must accompany your application. Renewals don't require it again.

2. Get the $7,500 surety bond. Florida law requires a four-year $7,500 bond. Because commissions in Florida are issued through the Governor's Office and applications are filed by bonding agencies, nearly everyone buys the bond and files in one package. The bond premium is small for the term.

3. File through the bonding agency with the $39 state fee. The agency bundles: your application, the education certificate, the bond, and the $39 state filing fee (this combined fee is what agencies quote; some sources itemize it differently — the state's own fee schedule has application and commission components).

4. Get commissioned, then buy your tools. Approval typically takes one to two weeks. Your commission certificate and required records arrive via the agency. Florida requires a notary seal meeting state specifications; a journal is not statutorily required for every act — but Florida notaries who do real estate or loan work should keep one anyway (and certain acts, like safe-deposit opening, require specific documentation).

Realistic total cost

Item Typical cost
3-hour education course ~$25 (often bundled)
State filing fee $39
$7,500 bond premium (4-year) ~$25–$50
Seal (required) + optional journal $20–$40
Realistic all-in ~$89–$99 (packages with E&O/stamps run higher)

The Florida-specific rules that matter

  • Residency is strict. Florida requires legal residency — snowbirds with out-of-state IDs can't qualify. This keeps supply lower and demand higher.
  • No exam — but the course covers the state's strict rules on identification and prohibited acts, and Florida disciplinary rules have real teeth.
  • Languages are a superpower here: Florida's market rewards bilingual notaries disproportionately, especially for loan-signing and legal-document volume work.

Timeline

Course (3 hours, same day) + bond and application (one session with an agency) → approval in one to two weeks → seal in a few days. Like Texas, Florida is a "commissioned within a month" state — among the fastest legitimate credentials in the country.

General information, not legal advice. Fees and rules change — verify with the Florida Department of State's notary section or your bonding agency before applying.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to become a notary in Florida?

A realistic all-in total is about $89–$99 for the required items: the $39 state filing fee, the mandatory 3-hour education course (~$25 or bundled), and the $7,500 bond premium — with extras like E&O insurance, journals and stamps pushing packages to $300+ if you add them.

Does Florida require an exam for notaries?

No exam. First-time applicants must complete a state-approved 3-hour interactive notary education course, but there is no written test. Renewals do not require the course again.

How long is a Florida notary commission?

Four years, renewable. The application, $7,500 bond, and education certificate are submitted through a bonding agency, which files with the state (the Governor's Office issues notary commissions in Florida).

How long does Florida notary approval take?

Typically around one to two weeks once the bonding agency has your completed application, course certificate, and bond — agencies file electronically in most cases. Peak periods and paper filings run slower.
