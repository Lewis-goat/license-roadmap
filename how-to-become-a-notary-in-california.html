How to Become a Notary in California (2026): Course, Exam, Cost

Last updated: September 13, 2026

California runs the most demanding notary process among the big states: a mandatory six-hour course, a real state exam, Live Scan background screening, and a $15,000 bond — and it's also one of the most lucrative markets in the country, which is why thousands of Californians still do it every year. Here's the exact path, with the verified numbers.

Step 1: Complete the mandatory 6-hour course

Every first-time applicant must complete a Secretary of State–approved six-hour course of study before appointment. Approved vendors (many online, many private) all teach the same state curriculum; prices run from about $20 (basic online) to $200 (premium packages). Renewing notaries who apply before their current commission expires can take a 3-hour refresher instead — miss the window and you're back to six hours.

Step 2: Pass the state exam (CPS HR)

California's notary exam is administered by CPS HR Consulting on a published schedule at test centers across the state. Key numbers:

  • $40 exam-and-application processing fee for new appointments and reappointments ($20 to retake a failed exam), paid by check or money order to the Secretary of State
  • Bring government-issued ID and your Proof of Completion certificate from the course
  • The exam tests California notary law — journal rules, identification standards, prohibited acts — and the pass bar is meaningful, so treat the course seriously

Step 3: Live Scan background check

California requires Live Scan fingerprinting for a state and federal background check — roughly $20–$125 depending on the provider and processing package. Bring the Live Scan form from your application packet to an approved fingerprinting location.

Step 4: Commission, bond, and oath — the 30-day rule

After passing, the state issues your commission packet. Two clocks start immediately:

  1. The $15,000 four-year surety bond (required by law) must be filed within 30 calendar days of the commission's start date — bond premiums run roughly $38–$100 for the term.
  2. Your oath of office files with the county clerk alongside the bond (county filing fees vary).

Miss the 30-day filing window and the process restarts. This is the single most common California notary failure mode.

Step 5: Your mandatory tools

California notaries must keep a sequential journal of every notarial act (more detailed than most states require) and use a seal meeting state specifications. Budget roughly $30–$60 for the pair.

Why Californians bother

The $15-per-act cap is the floor, not the story: California's population and real-estate volume make it one of the strongest loan-signing agent markets in the US, where trained notaries are paid per signing appointment rather than per signature. The barrier to entry (the exam) is exactly what keeps supply down.

General information, not legal advice. Fees and rules change — verify current requirements at the California Secretary of State's notary checklist and CPS HR exam page before paying anything.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to become a notary in California?

Realistic totals run roughly $313 to $595 all-in: a mandatory 6-hour education course (~$20–$200 depending on provider), a $40 exam-and-application fee ($20 to retake a failed exam), Live Scan fingerprinting (~$20–$125), the required $15,000 four-year bond (~$38–$100), plus a journal and seal.

Does California require an exam to become a notary?

Yes — unlike Texas and Florida, California requires a state-administered written exam through CPS HR Consulting, on top of the mandatory six-hour approved education course. Renewing notaries who file before their commission expires may take a three-hour refresher course instead of the full six hours.

How long does the California notary process take?

Plan on several weeks to a couple of months: course completion can be same-day online, the exam is offered on a published schedule at test centers statewide, and after passing there's the background check (Live Scan), the application, and the commission packet — then you must file your bond and oath within 30 calendar days of the commission's start date.

What does a California notary earn per notarization?

California caps the fee at $15 per notarial act, with separate higher allowances for certain services like depositions. Income beyond that comes from volume services such as loan signings, which pay per appointment rather than per signature.
